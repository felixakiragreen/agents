### F1 — `doctrine migrate` writes false `unrecorded` clause-fills, and the round-trip law says `ok`

**The most dangerous thing this charge found, and it found it by auditing its own tool's
output rather than trusting a clean line.** Every migrate rule fires off **one parse of the
pre-migration document**. On whiteboardy the head rules took the ledger from 5 parsed
entries to 127 — but `ledger.unrecorded-clauses` had already been computed against the
5-entry parse, so it appended `Decided: unrecorded.` / `Next: unrecorded.` into **61
entries that carry a real clause**, and the round-trip law printed `ok` for all of them
(`decided`/`next` are fields the rule *declares* it may change, so the assertion licenses
the damage — 016-F1's known blind spot, arriving in a new shape). Snappy showed the same
bug at a smaller scale, twice.

Repaired by hand in both buildings; the tool is unfixed and filed. Two cures to weigh:
re-parse between rule *classes* (structure rules, then field rules), or refuse to fill a
clause in an entry whose head this same run rewrote. Ancestors: 18c's "lint after
`--write`, never trust a clean round-trip line alone" and 18f's snappy finding — this is
that finding seen from the other side.

**The general lesson, for whoever runs migrate next: `round-trip ok` is a statement about
declared fields, not about meaning. Audit the diff.**

### F2 — the city's ledgers speak three house dialects the parser rejects and migrate cannot see

Three shapes, ~130 clauses, all repaired by hand here because no rule covers them and each
repair is a total, byte-preserving relocation:

- **`Decided (<scope>):` / `Next (<gate>):`** — whiteboardy 61, snappy 3. `parse` fails
  them; the repair is moving the colon to the field name (`Decided: (<scope>) …`).
- **Clauses buried mid-line** — snappy 41, five of them spelled `DECIDED:`. The clause
  exists in the prose; the repair is a newline.
- **Bullet-form inline ledgers** — rooted 30 entries, bob 46. A `## Ledger` section of
  `- **<date> · <mantle> (<tier>)** · …` bullets: the parser reads only the first bullet
  as a head and the rest as its body. The D63f grammar wants `---`-separated entries at
  line start, so the repair is a hoist (bodies byte-preserved, the `· decided … · next: …`
  tails lifted into their own clauses). **Proved the bullet form itself is the defect:** a
  bullet whose head is otherwise perfectly D63f still fails.

All three are mechanical enough to be migrate rules. Filed as a candidate for 026 or a
doctrine follow-on.

### F4 — rulings this charge made, so 030 and the next sweep inherit them

- **Glosses on a real charge id** (`16 (blessed)`, `SH5 merged (done)`, `07, 08 (09 folds
  in…)`) → the bare id, with the gloss preserved **verbatim** in the Status annotation as
  `· Depends-on note: …` (18g's sanctioned route). Where the gloss is a Felix precondition
  it becomes `⬡-gate: <text>` instead, attribution checked one by one. 28 cells in
  whiteboardy, 21 in snappy.
- **A date-range head** (`2026-08-22/23`, `2026-08-19/20`) → **the start date**, the span
  preserved in the head's own body. A session is dated when it begins. This settles 19's
  escalation E2, which said form alone could not pick.
- **A joint-session head** (`Architect … + Builder …`) → one mantle · one tier, the second
  session named in the body. D63f types one session per entry.
- **A model where a tier belongs** (`fable`, `opus-5`) → `unrecorded`, with the recorded
  word preserved verbatim in the body. 18f's waypoint-stepper precedent; a model is not a
  tier and inventing an effort is a fill.
- **`unstaffed` on a live charge** (024-F2's three) → `⬡-gate` where the cell's own words
  say it is Felix's call (waypoint-stepper 15, 16), `OPEN — DEFERRED` + the dissolved `—`
  where nobody is waiting (charge 023). D71 leaves exactly these two outs.
- **A cross-BUILDING dependency** (schema-migration M2 → `V-A`) → out of the column, into
  a `Depends-on note:` naming the other building — there is still no legal form, and
  charge 020 owns the question.
- **A live batch whose summons names the dead Dispatcher** → strike it dated, keep the
  instrument, name doctrine §10's interim tender. Done once, for Belvedere's batch 6.
  Closed batches' fences are history and stay verbatim.
