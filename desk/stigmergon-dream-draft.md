## The grave I'm building on

I built Belvedere in six days and I don't open it. A pretty, clumsy
prototype — and that's on me: I one-shotted it. I started at the end goal and
worked backwards, so the foundation was always the thing I specified least. I
told a session "add hover tooltips to everything" and got exactly that, and I
hated it — not their fault, mine, for under-specifying it. Taste cannot be
retrofitted. Details cannot be batched.

The fire hasn't changed since the first dream: thirty-plus terminals, me as
the scheduler, router, and intake clerk of my own city. The want was right.
The method was wrong. So this time the method is half the dream.

## The method is the dream

Stigmergon is built from the ground up: one step, nailed, then the next.
Every surface — a sidebar, a tooltip, a scrollbar — is designed with me in
its own sitting before a Builder touches it. Nothing ships un-designed, and
nothing ships untested: the camera and the probes come before the features,
because I refuse to spend my life bug-hunting my own instrument — the agents
who build this can see it and drive it, and every gesture is a test.

We already proved this works. The one part of Belvedere built this way — fake
claude, then engine, then fuzzer, then real physics, invariants all the way —
is the one part that survived. Stigmergon is that method applied to pixels.

And it is built to be rebuilt: modular enough that replacing a section never
threatens the building. I will change my mind about pieces of this. The
building will let me.

I am slowing down on purpose. When I get impatient — and I will — this
document is me telling us both: nail it, then move.

## What it is

My instrument for working with the Guild. Three panes — Context, Focus, Act —
and overlays above them. The Context pane is the city: every real repo,
discovered from disk, arranged by me — my names, my order, my nesting, my
colors, persisting over a truth that comes and goes beneath them. Agents live
in spaces; their homes start computed and move at my word.

Headless, this time. No connection to cmux — not a socket, not a pane, not
ever. Sessions run as events under the engine we salvaged; the Chat is how I
stand inside one; a terminal summon is the escape hatch. The census still
sees every live session wherever it runs — stigmergon just doesn't care what
a pane is. This replaces the terminal wall as my daily room.

And it is mine, foundationally. Every gesture is a named command; keys are
bindings I remap; ⌘? shows the registry itself, never a hand-written copy;
the whole city is walkable from the keyboard. Panes resize and remember.
Flows and summon presets are my data. And my hand reaches the board
directly — I park work, reorder it, edit it, without borrowing an Architect's
pen. Belvedere v1 was glass I couldn't act through; v2 grew thin controls
bolted on late. This one answers to me. The fence survives — an exhaustive
write list, everything else read-only — but the list is longer, because this
is a control room, not a window.

The first dream's wants all stand: the morning that starts at a rail of
batons instead of a wall of terminals; jump into anything, any account, live
or long dead; worktrees existing because I clicked. Images, the phone glance,
the closed laptop — later chapters, still named, still waiting.

## Fast, then alive

Fast is a foundation, not a feature: files to pixels in a blink, every
interaction under 16 ms, performance tested from day one — targets we chase,
not numbers we worship.

Alive is the horizon. There have been moments — stars aligned — when a dozen
agents were humming at once, and the only way to know it was to open their
chats one by one. I want to SEE it: one view, the whole hive working as a
collective. Maybe a small block stacking for every thousand tokens a session
generates; maybe it's the matrix with blocks falling instead of characters,
and at the bottom, the files being appended to. I don't know the final form
yet, and that's fine — it gets its own sitting like everything else. But we
instrument everything from day one, because when that view finally lights up,
I want it lit by real history, not mockups.

## The name

Stigmergy is already the Guild's law: trails directing agents, files carrying
the truth. Stigmergon is that law given a room. The name was the thesis all
along.

## What done feels like

I live in one window that is mine. Arranged by my hand, answering before I
notice I asked, every shortcut where I put it. The city on the left is my map
of everything real. The hive is visible — working, waiting, landed — and work
moves because I parked it, fired it, or answered it, in keys and clicks I
chose. And when the next surface is needed, we sit, we design it, we build it
once — and I never hate my own room again.
