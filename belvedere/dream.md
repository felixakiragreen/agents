# The Belvedere dream

*Felix Green, 2026-08-26. Drafted with my Mentat at the belvedere sitting and made
mine before it landed; the sitting's keel is [../plans/belvedere.md](../plans/belvedere.md).
The keel carries the analysis. This carries the fire.*

## The boiling point

I have 33 terminal sessions open and I can't keep going like this. I am the
scheduler, the router, and the intake clerk of my own city — every summons is my
keystroke, every handoff is my paste, every status check is me cycling through three
dozen windows trying to remember which one is thinking and which one has been
waiting on me since lunch. The terminal doesn't scale. I can't start a session and
close my laptop. I've been researching like a mad lad — superset, T3Code, my
friend's checklist app — and they all taught me the same two things: an interface
layer on top of the terminal is the right shape, and I don't want someone else's.
Their tools keep the truth in their databases. My truth lives in my repos — files
carry it, sessions coordinate through it, and the whole Guild runs on that law. I
want my own glass over my own city.

The summon rig was the first knuckle of this hand — four keystrokes to fire a
session, mantle and color and name-stamp riding along. Belvedere is the evolution:
not fire-one-session, but see-everything, fire-anything.

I had to look the name up: a belvedere is the structure built solely to command the
view. That's the whole brief in one word.

## What I want to see

**City View.** Every repo I'm working in, one glance: which buildings have live
crews, what's active right now. Sidebar down the left — Projects, Campaigns,
Windows — the same hierarchy the rig already stamps into every session name.

**Building pages.** Open a project and the files become panels: the overview, the
board, the ledger tail, the decisions waiting on my countersign, the ISSUES inbox.
The docs already answer a cold session's questions — I want them answering mine,
rendered, at a glance. Links everywhere: every row to its work doc, every session to
its resume, every finding to its evidence. One click beats a minute's hunt.

**Colors.** Mantle colors so I know who's who across the whole field — the rig
already knows them. Status colors so I know who needs me: working, idle,
escalated/blocked. The difference between a green city and a city with three red
windows is the difference between coffee and a fire drill, and I should know which
morning I'm having in under a second.

**Batons as buttons.** When a session ends it hands me a baton — the next summons,
verbatim. Today I carry that baton by hand: read it, fire the rig, paste the
summons. Hundreds of times. I want the baton to become a button. The city hands me
batons; I hand back fire. And when the baton is mine to run — a countersign, a
smoke, a taste ruling — it shows up as MY card, visually distinct, never auto-fired.
My mornings should start at a rail of batons, not a wall of terminals.

**Jump into anything.** Any session, any account, live or weeks dead — one click and
I'm standing in it. The transcripts are all on disk across three config dirs; stop
making me remember which hive a bee flew from.

**Worktrees without asking.** When a row needs a branch, the worktree and branch
exist because I clicked dispatch — not because I asked a session to please set up
its own workbench first.

**My own channel.** I want to whisper to the city without grabbing the Architect's
pen. A recurring way to hand down feedback, reorder priorities, defer things — my
gestures land as entries in the inbox, and the proper office applies them to the
board with my name on the ruling. My word travels; my hands stay off the stone.
That's not a workaround, that's the point: the fence between me and the file is what
keeps the city's records true.

## The fence I'm signing

Belvedere holds no truth. It reads the files and it fires the sessions — it never
edits a board, a ledger, a decision, the canon. If the glass shatters, the city
stands. Every dependency gets held to the same bar. And it shows me my own load —
usage across all three accounts, sessions in flight, WIP per board — because a
one-click dispatcher that hides the bill is how a sovereign DoS's himself.

## The horizon — and I know it's dessert

Eventually the City View becomes an actual city: a generative miniature, buildings
with real shape and history, windows lit by live sessions, the streets glowing
where the crews are working. I want to watch my city breathe. I want it badly
enough that I tried to put it first — saner heads prevailed. Build the spine: the
census, the batons, the glass. But log everything from day one, because the
miniature will be rendered from the history we're already writing, and when it
finally lights up I want it lit by real life, not by mockups.

The night shift is part of this too — one day a Steward works these boards while I
sleep, and its digest lands in this same glass. Belvedere is the throne room that
office will report into. Eyes first, then hands, then the night.

## What done feels like

I open one window instead of thirty-three. I glance and I know: what's running,
what landed while I was gone, what's blocked on me. The batons are buttons and I
fire the day in clicks, not pastes. Any session from any hive, one click to stand
inside it. Images, the closed laptop, the phone glance, the glowing miniature —
later chapters, named and waiting.

Three hives, one city — and finally a room to see it from. Build me the room.
