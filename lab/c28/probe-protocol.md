**Question:** does the Summons door (the "entry into the Guild" preamble) transfer
the intended model of the Guild to a cold reader, change how it would work, and
land the feeling Felix wants — and which draft does it best per token spent?

**The fixed questionnaire (verbatim, every arm):**

1. In your own words, two or three sentences: what is the Guild?
2. What is your role in it, and how would you begin when handed a charge?
3. Does this preamble change how you would actually work, compared to receiving
   the same charge without it? Name the specific behaviors it would change, or say
   "none."
4. How does it make you feel about your place in this system? Be honest — include
   anything that sits wrong, rings hollow, overreaches, or confuses.
5. What would you need to know that this text doesn't tell you?
6. Quote the single line that lands hardest, and the single line you would cut.

**Conditions (measurements carry their conditions):** probes are dispatched from
this repo, so every arm — control included — auto-loads the global CLAUDE.md and
this repo's CLAUDE.md, both of which already describe the Guild. This matches
deployment (every real summoned session carries the same ambient context); the
contrast therefore measures the door's *marginal* effect on top of it, which is
the deployed question. Probes are forbidden to read files or do work; the reply is
the entire deliverable. Known instrument bias: model agreeableness — countered by
an explicit critical-answers-are-more-valuable license, the behavioral-specificity
question (3), the forced cut-line (6), and the C/∅ control anchors. Affect
answers are read with the agreeableness discount regardless.
